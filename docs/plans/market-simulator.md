# Plan: the market simulator

Written for: the Opus session that runs this batch in Claude Code on this repo. The user's decisions were taken in conversation on 2026-11-08; the scenario list was widened by the `strategic-planner` (Fable) and checked against SPEC by Opus. Nothing in §1 is open for redesign. The spec merge (SPEC §8.10, SCENARIOS' simulator section, `tests/pending_scenarios.txt`) is this batch's first milestone, not this commit.

**Why.** Several milestones wait on 14 days of real data (about 2026-11-21): M33b's data-bound parts, M33c (K8) and M34. The simulator lets their code be built and tested before then, and, for the first time, checks what the analysis infers (sold, expired, cancelled, relisted) against a known truth, and what the plan decides against what would have made money. On 2026-11-21 only the constants are set from real data.

## 1. What is decided (do not re-litigate)

1. **No production change.** The simulator writes raw envelopes exactly as `ahcloud/collector.py` stores them (`raw_key`, `raw_envelope`: Blizzard's record shapes, commodities and regular auctions in separate bodies, Blizzard's band bounds `LONG` 2–12 h and `VERY_LONG` over 12 h) into a local store folder. A runner calls `ahcloud.hourly.run_hourly` per snapshot and `ahcloud.daily.run_daily` once per simulated day, in-process, with the catalog's item search answered by a fake client over `FakeTransport` (as `tests/support/cloud_world.py` does). `now_ms` is the newest snapshot's `at_ms`, so simulated time needs no clock change.
2. **Everything runs locally**: no AWS, no WoW, no network, no Blizzard credentials. X1 measures this machine's wall time and peak memory as the trend against Lambda's 15 min and 2 GB; a real Lambda run, if wanted, is asked first.
3. **Ground truth.** Every post, buy (units and price), cancel, expiry and relist, ours included, goes to a truth log beside the raw. Scorers compare against it.
4. **Three tiers plus stress.** Tier 1 (`SU`, unit sequences, exact numbers a human can check) and Tier 2a (`SM`, inference against truth within a stated tolerance) are pytest tests. Tier 2b (`SD`, decision quality) **starts as a report**, not a failing test; a stable one may become a test later, the user's call. Tier 3 (`SX`, stress) is run by hand or with `-Bench`, never in CI.
5. **Paper trading.** Synthetic characters (gold, bags, professions) follow the plan inside the simulated market: their buys take listings, their posts appear in later snapshots, competitors react. Their state reaches AHSync **through the real addon import**: `tests/support/savedvars_builder.py` is extended to write a day's posts, purchases, mail, inventory and loot as SavedVariables, and `state/importer.py` reads them, so the importer, cost basis, tracking and missed-peak logic run as in life. Direct database writes are kept only as a fallback for SX6 if import speed is the bottleneck.
6. **Benchmarks.** Each decision run is scored against an **oracle** (knows the future: the upper bound), **do nothing** (zero) and a **naive trader** (buys the cheapest listing, reposts at `med7`). Metrics: gold made, gold-days tied up, worst drawdown, stranded stock at the end, share of the oracle's profit; a ledger conservation check after every event (SU12).
7. **Determinism.** One `random.Random(seed)` per run, no other randomness; the same seed gives byte-identical raw, truth and report (SU9, SPEC §8.6). Failures print the seed.
8. **Fitting.** The model's parameters (listing price spread against the floor, arrivals per hour, stack sizes, duration mix, commodity cancel share, non-cheapest-first fills 0.2%) are fitted from the raw collected so far, then shaped per scenario. Constants of the engine are **never** tuned on simulated data: the simulator ranks strategies; SPEC's constants still come from real data (M33b step 1, M34 step 1).
9. **Momentum (SD1–SD4) starts as failing tests that document the gap.** M34 as specced makes the detection half pass (the regime label); the action half needs **M34b** (decision 14).
10. **The recent-activity multiplier** (`docs/plans/blizzard-cloud.md` §1, deferred item 1) is wanted. It weighs the last hours' observed endings against those expected for that hour of the week, acting only outside chance, so a busy item reacts in hours and a quiet hour that is normal for its slot does not trigger it. SD5 and SD7 are its tests; it is its own milestone with its spec merge.
11. **Reaction targets** (the pass bands of the decision report; provisional, revisited with real data):

    | Scenario | Target |
    |---|---|
    | SD5, a real drop of ≥ 40% in sold/day that lasts | no new buys within **12 h** for an item selling ≥ 100 a day, **36 h** at about 25 a day, **4 days** at about 10 a day (until then the 7-day average decides); units bought after the drop ≤ one day of the new rate; price cuts on held stock from day 3 |
    | SD7, one quiet day (≤ 50% dip) then normal | no selling below the plan; buying shrinks by at most 25% for one day |
    | SD28 / SD29, a held item's price drops ≥ 20% | the exit rule fires when the drop lasts 12 h; nothing changes for a one-snapshot dip (FM5) |
    | SD3, a spike lasting under 24 h | zero buys; if holding, sell into it |
    | SD1, a real rise | labelled within 5 days (M34's provisional ±1%/day band) |

12. **A solid steady deal** (SD9, SD10): **commodities** ≥ 100 sold a day; **crafted gear** ≥ 10 sold a day (per item key; crafted gear has fixed stats, so suffix keys don't split it); both ≥ 15% margin after the AH cut and deposits, ≥ 60% sell chance at the planned price, price and sales within ±15% of their 14-day level. **Revisit on real data on 2026-11-21.** Pass: the planted deal is in its category's top 5 (Gathering's gold per hour, or the plan's profit per day) within 2 days of data, its realized margin within 5 points of the planned one, and no SD11 decoy is funded.

The switch is a spec departure approved by the user at that step. SM10/SM11 are its test pair.

Under "rising, demand holding" the 30-day cap of TT22 loosens by a bounded amount and a staged exit (sell part into the rise, hold the rest past 7 days) becomes plannable; under "falling" or "demand fading" buying shrinks. Its numbers come from the SD1–SD4 report; its spec merge is the user's approval.

23. **The overlap split is fixed first, on its own (BZ44; Opus at execution, within decision 21).** Selling and expiring compete, weighed by the item's early-leave rate measured over the whole observed life in the same lifetimes pass (5 pseudo leaves toward the pooled rate). Across six simulated market shapes the net misfiling fell from +3–6% of units sold to +1–2%. A rate measured only in the last 3, 6 or 12 hours before the window was tried: about a point better for commodities, worse for gear, and unsteady between cutoffs, so not worth a constant. The remaining +1–2% comes from listings selling less often late in life. **Corrected at the independent review (same day):** a rate measured over the whole pass let snapshots after a day change that day's endings, which `id_days` never recomputes, and made an hour pooled across midnight depend on recompute order; the rate is now causal (the gaps that ended in the 24 h before each ending), the pass's lookback grew from 36 h to 66 h to hold that window, the cloud keeps 5 days of compact snapshots instead of 4, an item without exposure takes its pool's rate, `Ending.overlap` keeps the raw share for `tools/lifetimes_report.py`'s outcome and relist counts, and the pools count every pass snapshot's commodities. Two regression tests with a non-zero rate (both failed before). A rebuild over the 31 real snapshots: 173 s (171 s before), peak 1,047 MB (871 MB); the longer lookback's full cost shows only once a market has more than three days of raw, which SX1 measures. The unseen-sales estimator follows as its own research step (decision 21's (b)): the first candidate recovered 27–90% of the unseen units depending on the market shape, and the unseen share reached 35% in a busy market.
24. **The unseen-sales estimate is "A", the thin-supply flag is deferred (the user, 2026-11-08).** Three estimators were compared over six market shapes with real-shaped seller pricing (the collected raw: 42% of new commodity listings at the cheapest price, 19% up to 5% above, 38% above that, 1% below): A (a listing's first observed gap's sales, extended back over half its posting gap) took the error from −4% to −0.6% (steady), −9% to −4% (busy) and −3% to +2% (flooded); two demand-rate estimators overshot or fell back to the observed count; stack-size inference was dropped (first-seen quantities are mostly 1–5). No observable signal separated the shapes where sales go unseen (18%) from the rest, and those shapes may be an artifact of the seller model (the floor drifting above most buyers), so the flag waits for MS2's fitted markets. Built as BZ45 (`engine/unseen.py`, credited to the measuring gap so the BZ44 review's invariants hold; `ID_METHOD_VERSION` 8). **SM1 stays pending:** with MS1's own sellers (about 17% undercut the cheapest) sold/day is still 1–19% low over a week, from listings bought out before any snapshot sees them, which no snapshot estimator can count; whether real sellers undercut that often is what fitting must answer (the 1% seen undercutting is a floor: undercutters sell before they are seen). **The estimate is capped at once a listing's first-gap sales** (the user, 2026-11-08, after the BZ45 review: a long posting gap after missed refreshes multiplied one buyout into hundreds of phantom units; the cap changes nothing at the hourly cadence, where the factor is about ½). The item detail's "about N a day unseen (est.)" line is the next small milestone (a stored column, a summary field, the mockup). A rebuild over the 31 real snapshots: 185 s, peak 1,223 MB (1,047 MB with BZ44, 871 MB before); the analyzer has 2 GB.

27. **MS2's first findings (2026-11-08; decided in 28 and 29).** (a) **Linen's remaining 2.5% is the V41 sales-rate prior**: on a complete window the document equals the lifetimes' count ÷ (days + 2/24) with 1/day added, so a busy item's sold/day is pulled down by 2 h ÷ the hours observed, 1.2% over 7 days and 2.4% over 3.4; the sales-profile weighting of a partial day adds ±1.5% noise; nothing else. (b) **The 31 real hours** (`fit.observe`): commodity new listings sit 0.8% under the cheapest price at first sight, 42.8% at it, 18.9% up to 5% above; durations 10% 2 h, 77% 8 h, 13% 24 h (MS1 assumed 60% 24 h); real Linen sells about 52,000 units a day from 2,450 new listings, with only 42 units at its floor (86 within 5%), so its floor covers about 2.4 minutes of sales; the unseen estimate is 25–28% of sold for busy cloth; commodity cancels are 6.0% of endings market-wide. (c) **MS1's seller model can't be fitted to that.** Pricing only off the current floor ratchets the floor up whenever the cheapest auction sells; an anchor (`Item.anchor`) stops the ratchet, but no grid of posting rate, buyer rate and buyer price level reproduces the real sold : expired of 2 : 1 at the real book size (the simulated markets sell under 1 : 1), and no true undercut share shows the real 0.8% under the floor (at most 0.33%). (d) **For a busy commodity the observed sales are not monotone in true demand**: more demand sells more listings before any snapshot sees them, so proportional calibration diverges; markets that fit the real observables about equally well put the engine at 35–81% of the true sales. (e) **A thin-supply signal exists in the simulated markets**: over 72 markets, floor depth in minutes of sales correlates with the undercount (r 0.45 against log engine ÷ truth); under 60 minutes the engine counted 6–51% of true sales, above it a median 57%. SM1 stays pending; the flag is not built.

29. **The V41 prior stays; the thin-supply flag is its own milestone (the user, 2026-11-08).** The prior's pull on a busy item (2 h ÷ hours observed) is specified (SPEC §3.3.1's 7-day example) and small next to the unseen error, so it is kept, and SM1 applies it to the truth; revisit with a cutoff on hours observed if outages prove common. The flag says "sales may be undercounted", with no number, when the units within 5% of a commodity's cheapest price would last less than one snapshot interval of its sales (60 min at the hourly cadence): the cutoff is the collector's cadence, not a constant fitted on simulated data (decision 8). Over the 31 real hours it marks 85 of 761 commodities with sales (11%), 70 of the 169 selling 1,000+ a day (41%); gear is never marked. 31. **SM1 at ±8% over three seeds; u(p) measured before posting, in M33b (the user, 2026-11-08).** SM1 (the steady-market sales test) runs seeds 1–3 against ±8%: the excess is always high and mostly SU2's (a cancelled cheapest auction counts sold), which the API cannot tell apart. Tried first in SM2's worlds at the post-now position 0.98: with bots 0.15–0.18 → 0.26–0.28 against a true 0.53–0.61; without bots 0.11–0.14 → 0.18–0.24 against 0.35–0.36; real-shaped 0.02–0.05 → 0.05 against 0.06–0.14; it recovers about half, the rest being undercutters bought before any snapshot. 32. **A confirmed floor for the post price (the user, 2026-11-08).** The post price stops undercutting a lone cheap listing: it prices off the cheapest price that has at least N units or K auctions within a few % of it (the API names no seller, so auctions and units stand in for sellers). N, K and the band come from real data; the rule is measured first in the simulator (a lone-cheap-listing world beside SM10/SM11) and built in the sold-price fix milestone (§1.13), which reopens the post price anyway. Nothing is built for it in MS2.
33. **The relist estimator divides by the estimated early leavers (the user, 2026-11-08).** Listings that vanish in their last hour really expired 77% of the time for 2 h auctions, 92% for 8 h and 94–97% for 24 h (SM4's worlds). A weight on those endings (the user's duration rule) dilutes, since only an early leaver can have a real match; so every ending's match counts, and the excess ÷ (1 − placebo) is divided by Σ (1 − its BZ44 expired share), which needs no new constant. Mean gap over 9 cases: 2.4 points with the real duration mix (dropping last-hour endings 3.2, the duration rule 2.8, before 5.4). `tools/lifetimes_report.py` also counts the endings in their last hour. SM4 passes on seeds 1 and 2; seed 3 reads 6.3 points low at both rates, untraced, for M33b's relist question.
34. **SU12 restated (MS3, Opus; a scenario row whose formula proved wrong).** The ledger's identity is cash + bags + listed + deposits held + mailbox gold + mailbox items, stock at cost, = start + realized gains − AH cuts − deposits lost; the row's first form left out the realized gains, so it could not hold once anything sold above its cost. Costs move proportionally and exactly (the last unit of a lot carries the remainder), so the identity holds to the copper after every event. A sale returns its share of the deposit with the gold; an expiry or a cancel loses it (§1.16).
35. **The oracle (MS3, Opus; the plan named it but did not define it).** In time order, every sale to others is filled first from the cheapest units listed in the day before whose price beats the sale's after the 5% cut, then from the characters' starting stock (which never ages out); each unit is used once. It ignores capital, deposits and its own effect on buyers, so it is an optimistic yardstick, not a proven maximum; MS4 may refine it once the SD rows show where it misleads. The naive trader buys the cheapest other listing whole when reposting at the document's `med7` pays after the cut and the 24 h deposit, and posts everything it holds at `med7` for 24 h.
36. **How the paper trade runs (MS3).** A daily login at 10:00 UTC (an hour after the cloud's daily run): mail collected (a sale's gold an hour after the sale, returned items at once; postage not modelled), the SavedVariables written by `tests/support/savedvars_builder.savedvars_text` from the characters' day and parsed by the real reader, then `apply_data`, `tracking_pass` and `replan_all` with the newest document, as `__main__` does; the characters buy and vendor at once, post at once or at the window's start (if the action is still pending then), and mail at once. Crafts, disenchants and holds are counted as undone. The starter world (`decision.starter`: stock to sell and a 3,000-unit Silk dump at 45% of `med7`) scores the plan at 61% of the oracle on seeds 1 and 2 (it sold its stock; 1.96 and 1.98 g), the naive trader −5.2 and −6.4 g (it bought the dump and stranded about 2,500 units), nothing 0. **Finding for MS4:** the plan funded no flip of the dump on its 4-day-old market; why (thin windows, the forecast, the dump being one stack) is SD9's first question. The dump also expired at 08:50, before the 10:00 login could have bought it.
37. **Why the plan passed on MS3's Silk dump (MS4, Opus; traced 2026-11-09; the engine's side is the user's).** Three causes, each shown by instrumenting the plan: (a) **the trust gate**: a flip bought under the item's p10 is a Buy for later (`candidates.py`), and its selling window needs 3 weeks of data (`PATTERN_WEEKS`, SPEC §3.4D.2, PF28), so on a market under 3 weeks old every batch is excluded as "Forecast too uncertain (1 week of data)"; the same dump, 150 stacks of 20 at 75 c against a `med7` of 165 c, is bought on day 21 of a 28-day world (23 purchases, all resold in its windows, 0.70 g made, nothing stranded) and not on day 3 of an 8-day one; (b) **whole auctions**: a flip buys whole auctions, and one 3,000-unit auction exceeds every window's capacity (1,545 units), so it never becomes a candidate, though Forever lets a buyer take any number of a commodity auction's units; (c) **the harness**: MS3's dump expired at 08:50, before the 10:00 login, while the daily document (built from the 08:20 snapshots) still listed it. The trader now takes the hourly documents (`RunSettings.hourly`, `Run.published`) and logs in at chosen hours (`login_hours`).
38. **The trust gate stays; commodity flips buy in lots (the user, 2026-11-09).** (a) A dip flip on a market under 3 weeks old stays excluded ("Forecast too uncertain"), so the SD worlds that test buying run 4 weeks or more in the report; their pytest runs stay short and check the report line (decision 4). (b) A flip buys a commodity's auctions in lots of the reference stack, each a batch funded within the risk budget and the character's spendable gold (§3.4D.1), built as R30 in its own PR (#77).

## 2. Working rules

CLAUDE.md's, plus:

- **Where the code lives**: `tools/sim/` (standard library only), mirrored by `tests/tools/sim/`. One module per component: `market.py` (the model), `emulator.py` (snapshots and raw), `truth.py`, `runner.py` (the cloud job in-process), `trader.py` (our agent and the import), `scorers.py`, `fit.py`, `inference.py` (the SM rows' measures); `tools/sim_report.py` prints the inference and decision reports.
- **Scenario first**: every SU, SM, SD and SX row is in SCENARIOS before its code, its ID in `tests/pending_scenarios.txt` until built. Test names follow CLAUDE.md (`test_SU3_commodity_cancel_while_cheaper_stays`).
- **Tolerance tests** (Tier 2a) state their tolerance and the truth they are compared with in the row's `why`; SPEC §8.10 says when a tolerance is allowed (only against a simulated truth, never for an engine formula, which keeps exact scenarios).
- **Small worlds in pytest** (about 50 items, 3–14 days, under a few seconds each); full-size worlds only in SX.
- **A failing simulated scenario is a finding, not a bug, until the truth model is checked.** A gap between engine and truth is triaged: the simulator wrong, a known limit (pinned by a scenario), or an engine bug (CLAUDE.md's bug rule: reproducing scenario, fix, SPEC, LESSONS).
- **Spec contradictions found while planning** are fixed in the spec merge: SPEC §2.4 "sold/day … per item over 30 days" against §3.3.2 and §3.3.6 and the code (`analysis/market.py`: the 7-day volume window): the 7-day average is what is built; the blend cap (§3.3.2 "never more than half", §2.4's planned 0.9 at 300 listings) and the tier count (four in §3.3.2, five in §2.4) are marked as built against planned for M33b. SM5 takes both as parameters.
